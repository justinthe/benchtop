# Why the Moon has phases

The Moon doesn't make its own light and it isn't changing shape. Half of it is always lit by the Sun; what changes
is how much of that lit half we can see from Earth as the Moon goes round us once a month.

I'd like people to drag the Moon around its orbit and watch the phase change, with the Sun on one side as a lamp.
Show what the Moon looks like from Earth at each point (new, crescent, quarter, gibbous, full) and say how much of
it is lit, and how many days into the month it is. Maybe let them tilt their head to Earth's view.

Keep it to the classic classroom picture: a lamp, a ball for the Moon, and a head-sized Earth. No eclipses (the
orbit is tilted, so they don't happen every month). A synodic month is about 29.5 days.
